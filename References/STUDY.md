read from 24-frame sheets by 4 agents, then compared with 373, 374, 382, 365 by one more. Videos are not in git.

## Patterns

- Something fills the space outside the picture, or there is no space outside the picture: 16 of 17. Twelve are full-bleed (5 square, 2 at 4:5, 2 at 3:4, 3 at 9:16), 3 put a title in the band above the picture (V9K6, 2Z55, Dk4e) and 1 puts a blurred copy plus narration there (01AJW). Only JTgG leaves plain black bars, like ours.
- On-screen text that gives context beyond the dialogue (a title bar, premise label, post card, narrator line or 'In the end' caption): 8 of 17 (HnqrB, X4kny, gy9f, 01AJW, SfcfV, V9K6, 2Z55, Dk4e). That is 7 of the 11 TV/film/cartoon references. Tko praised this by name on three of them: V9K6 'prompt at top', 2Z55 'fake twitter post', HnqrB 'gives context'.
- A title that stays on screen the whole time, above the picture: 5 of 17 (SfcfV, V9K6, 2Z55, Dk4e, and gy9f part of the time). All are white or black bold text with 1-2 keywords in orange or red. Most are written as a tease that the clip pays off ('One Answer Was All He Needed...', 'Some Days You Wake Up To Guns / And It's Still Your Lucky Day').
- Captions sit mid-frame, about 45-70% down the picture, not at the bottom edge: 11 of 17. The 6 lower-third cases (01AJW, mIOt, Sm_0, JTgG, 2Z55, Dk4e) are mostly the film's own subtitle style.
- Captions are bold sans with an outline, larger than a thin subtitle: 15 of 17. 11 of 17 are uppercase. The film references Tko praised for their subtitles (HnqrB, 01AJW, 5OUl) use full lines in 1-2 rows or short word chunks. Neither is a thin serif.
- More than 1s of picture after the last word: 12 of 17. 9 of 17 hold 3s or more (HnqrB 5.0, gy9f 6.7, 5OUl 3.25, mIOt 3.8, 2Z55 3.3, JTgG 6.6, X6hp 4.5, II1F 4.15, Dk4e 9.1). Only 5 cut within 1s, and those cut on a reveal or punchline line (X4kny, Sm_0, SfcfV, tyik, e9NR).
- The ending is a wordless visual beat (a reaction face, a resolution shot, an action payoff or an outro montage): about 10 of 17 (HnqrB, 5OUl, mIOt, JTgG, 2Z55, V9K6, sUeJ, X6hp, II1F, Dk4e).
- The first word is at or near 0s: about 13 of 17. The 4 that open on picture first (mIOt 8.6s, SfcfV 3.0s, Sm_0 about 1.5s, 5OUl 0.5s) open on a violent, tense or face-reveal image.
- The story is spliced across scenes, keeping whole exchanges and dropping what lies between them: 6 of 17 (HnqrB, X4kny, gy9f, V9K6, Sm_0, II1F). Of those, HnqrB and V9K6 stay inside one film, so the setup and the payoff come from the same source.
- Captions in asterisks or brackets for sounds and silent action ('*car rolls up*', '*SEES GUN*', '*NODS NO*', '*LAUGHS*'): 4 of 17 (5OUl, mIOt, sUeJ, II1F). Tko praised the subtitles on 5OUl for exactly this.
- Inserted cutaway shots that illustrate a line (a scene-setting insert, a clue close-up, B-roll on the key noun): 6 of 17 (HnqrB, Sm_0, X6hp, e9NR, II1F, tyik).
- Swears are starred in the captions: 5 of 17. We already do this.
- The climax beat turns black and white or desaturated: 2 of 17 (2Z55, which Tko praised as 'edits make it more dramatic', and 01AJW).
- Flash frames, glitch cuts or shots under 0.5s used as an effect: 4 of 17 (sUeJ, II1F, tyik, Sm_0). All but Sm_0 are streamer, IRL or MMA clips.
- Speech fills a median of 65% of the runtime. The references with the lowest shares (Sm_0 0.23, mIOt 0.33, Dk4e 0.39, 5OUl 0.55, JTgG 0.44) give that time to reactions, inserts and silent payoffs, not to dead air between lines.

## Ranked changes

### [1] Pick the ending on purpose. If the scene has a visual button (a reaction face, a resolution shot, a character walking off, a gag payoff), hold 2-4s past the last word, up to the next source cut. Keep a hard cut (under 0.8s) only when the last line is itself the reveal or punchline.
- **Evidence:** 9 of 17 references hold 3s or more (HnqrB 5.0, 5OUl 3.25, mIOt 3.8, 2Z55 3.3, JTgG 6.6, and others). The median tail is 3.25s; ours is 0.38s. Tko praised 5OUl for 'ending on a funny clip with no talking'. Our own 374, with a 10.84s tail, is one of his two best. 365 currently ends on a blurry empty frame, which shows that the 0.3s rule picks bad last frames. The hard-cut references (X4kny 'What?', SfcfV 'lucky', tyik) all end on a reveal line.
- **Fits us:** Yes. Sitcoms and cartoons run on reaction shots: Larry's stare, Rick's smirk, Summer's face. No copyright or misleading risk. One risk: dead air if the held shot has no reaction in it, so cap the tail at about 4.5s and require a source cut or a visible change in it.
- **How:** Picking step: add end_mode = 'button' | 'hard' per clip, plus a button_end time the picker chooses from the transcript and shot list (the first source cut 1.5-4.5s after the last word that lands on a face or an action). Render: set trim end to button_end. Keep a short audio fade (afade out 0.3s) so the music or ambience doesn't click. Default to 'button' unless the last line is tagged as a reveal.

### [1] Use the empty top bar for a two-line title that stays on screen the whole clip: white bold italic sans, with 1-2 hook words in orange, written as a tease that the clip's last beat pays off.
- **Evidence:** There is a title above the picture in V9K6 (Tko: 'prompt at top helps tell story'), SfcfV, 2Z55 (Tko: 'fake twitter post to add a prompt'), Dk4e and gy9f, which makes 5 of 17. Context text of some kind appears in 8 of 17, including 7 of the 11 film refs. SfcfV's title word 'lucky' comes back in the last line. Our top 14% is pure black in every clip.
- **Fits us:** Strong fit: it fixes the problem that a viewer who hasn't seen the episode lacks context, without narration audio. Examples: 'He Hid a Virus in the Memory / He Let Them Steal' for 373, 'Larry Found a Way / To Ruin Jeff's Wordle Streak' for 365. Risk: clickbait that the clip doesn't deliver hurts retention and the channel's trust, so the title must describe what is actually in the clip. Don't use show logos or anything that looks like an official channel. If we ever try a post-card style (2Z55), use our own channel name only.
- **How:** Picking step outputs title_line1, title_line2 and highlight_words (one cheap field, no extra pass). Render: an ASS subtitle style for the top bar (bold italic sans, about 64px, white, with orange override tags on the keywords), centred at rows 0.05-0.13, applied through ffmpeg's subtitles filter for the clip's full length. Keep it inside the bar so it never covers the picture.

### [2] Redo the caption style: bold sans with a black outline about 4-6px, larger (about 56-64px), placed about 60% down the picture band rather than at its bottom edge. Keep full dialogue lines in 1-2 rows for story clips.
- **Evidence:** 15 of 17 references use bold outlined sans. 11 of 17 sit mid-frame, 45-70% down. The film references Tko praised for 'not cutting words' and story (HnqrB, 01AJW) keep full dialogue lines rather than one word at a time. Our thin serif with only a shadow washes out on light frames: 373 row 3 (beige and yellow glow), 374 (on Rick's white coat).
- **Fits us:** Yes. Readability is the issue, not the style. Mid-band placement also stops captions from sitting on the edge between the strip and its blurred copy on cartoon shared shots. Low risk. Avoid word-by-word neon: that is the streamer look.
- **How:** Render only: change the ASS style (FontName to a bold sans such as Arial Black or Montserrat ExtraBold, Outline 5, Shadow 0, MarginV set so the baseline sits about 0.60-0.65 of the frame height). No change to the picking step.

### [2] Add captions in asterisks for silent beats and sound cues that carry the plot, such as '*shoots the portal gun*', '*car rolls up*', '*stares*', '*Rick dances*'. Use a different colour (yellow italic) so they read as stage directions.
- **Evidence:** 4 of 17 do this (5OUl, mIOt, sUeJ, II1F). Tko praised 5OUl's 'subtitles that tell the story', and those are the '*SEES GUN*' and '*NODS NO*' captions. It pairs with change 1: the longer silent endings need a caption so they read on mute.
- **Fits us:** Good fit for sitcom and cartoon gags, which often turn on a look or an action. Keep it to 0-3 per clip, or it turns into narration. No copyright risk.
- **How:** Picking step: an optional list of {t_start, t_end, text} for silent beats, written in the same pass as the cut list. Render: the same ASS file with a second style (yellow, italic) at the same position as the dialogue.

### [3] Stop trimming every reaction and pause out of the middle of the clip. Aim for 65-75% speech, giving the time back to reaction shots and silent gag beats.
- **Evidence:** The reference median is 65% speech; ours are 0.86, 0.86 and 0.76. Every reference under 0.6 (Sm_0, mIOt, 5OUl, JTgG) gives its silence to reactions and payoffs, not dead air. HnqrB is at 0.78 and still 'lets beats breathe'. 374, at 0.62, is our best.
- **Fits us:** Fits comedy: a reaction beat after the joke is part of the joke (Curb especially). Risk: it slows the pacing, so keep a reaction only if it lands on a face. Never keep empty frames. The splice-the-whole-story memory still holds: cut tangents, not reactions.
- **How:** Picking rule: when splicing, keep the source shot that follows a punchline line up to 1.5s even if nobody speaks. Report speech_share in the metrics and flag clips above 0.80 for a second look.

### [3] Add context text in two short spots only: a premise label in the first 3s when the title isn't enough, and an optional closing 'In the end...' line over the resolution shot when the episode resolves off-clip.
- **Evidence:** X4kny's top-left 'FRANK'S LIVER HAS SUFFERED SEVERE DAMAGE.' for about 3s; HnqrB's 'Management wants to transfer her...' and 'In the end, Friendship 7 landed safely' (Tko: 'gives context'); 01AJW's narration line (Tko: 'tells story, no left out parts').
- **Fits us:** Fits recaps and mid-episode clips where the setup happened earlier in the episode (373's virus plan, 382's hostage setup). Risk: wrong facts are worse than none, and each line costs picker tokens, so use at most 2 per clip and only when the transcript supports them. Don't use a running narration band on every beat.
- **How:** Picking step: optional premise_label and outro_line. Render: ASS events. The label goes in the bottom black bar or as a white box with black uppercase text at the top-left of the picture for 0-3s; the outro line is in the caption style over the last 2-3s.

### [4] When a line refers to something shown elsewhere in the same episode, cut a 1-2s insert of it over the audio (an L-cut), or pop in a still with rounded corners.
- **Evidence:** HnqrB uses scene-setting inserts over narration lines. e9NR, X6hp and II1F (Tko: 'uses other clips to help tell the story') cut to B-roll on the noun being said. tyik pops in a photo on 'went to the army'. That is 6 of 17.
- **Fits us:** Fits when the episode has the shot, e.g. 374 'In 1998 they had this promotion' over the Mulan or McDonald's shot, or a callback gag. Use footage from the same episode only. Don't pull outside B-roll; that is the interview and MMA habit, and it raises the copyright risk. Overuse makes it feel cheap.
- **How:** Picking step: optional inserts [{at, src_start, src_end}]. Render: ffmpeg overlay or concat that swaps the video stream for the insert range while keeping the main audio continuous, through the same band and crop pipeline.

### [4] Turn the single climax beat black and white (or heavily desaturated) for 1-3s.
- **Evidence:** 2Z55 (Tko: 'uses edits to make it more dramatic') and 01AJW's last gag beat, 2 of 17. It is a cheap visible edit, which meets the memory rule that every clip needs visible editing.
- **Fits us:** Fits the dramatic and dark-comedy beats: the gunshot in 382, Rick's betrayal in 373. Use it once per clip at most and not on every clip, or it becomes a gimmick.
- **How:** Picking step: an optional climax_bw {start, end}. Render: hue=s=0 (or eq=saturation=0.15) with enable='between(t,a,b)' on the picture chain.

### [5] For tension clips, open on the most intense or violent frame of the scene for 0.5-1.5s before the first line, then start the dialogue.
- **Evidence:** mIOt flashes the throat grab within 2s, before any words. 5OUl opens on a 0.5s close-up of Dexter. Sm_0 cuts to the elbow clue within 3s. That is 4 of 17 that open on the picture first. Most others start on the first word, which we already do.
- **Fits us:** Only for drama and suspense clips, not sitcom banter, where the first line is the hook. Risk: if the frame isn't in the clip's own story, it reads as misleading, so take it from the same scene.
- **How:** Picking step: an optional cold_open {src_start, src_end}, 0.5-1.5s, prepended with a hard cut. Render: concat before the main range; the captions shift by its length.

## Skip

- Flash and glitch frames, median shots of 0.03s, and white flash transitions (sUeJ, II1F, tyik): these are the streamer, IRL and MMA edit language. On a sitcom or cartoon scene they look cheap and fight the source's own cuts.
- Word-by-word neon captions with a different coloured keyword per chunk (gy9f, X6hp, tyik, sUeJ, II1F): the hype look of podcasts, streams and MMA. For story clips, the references Tko praised (HnqrB, 01AJW, V9K6) keep the captions clean.
- B-roll from outside the source (NBA footage, stock street footage, UFC press conferences in X6hp, e9NR, II1F): our clips are cut from one episode, and outside footage adds copyright exposure and breaks the story.
- Smash cuts between unrelated sources that make up a joke the source never had (II1F): misleading for a show clip, and it can't be done inside one episode anyway.
- Montages of a character across seasons (gy9f, X4kny across episodes): good formats, but they need several episodes. Revisit only if we ever cut across a season.
- Square, 4:5 and 3:4 full-bleed frames: 12 references use them, but they get letterboxed in the Shorts player anyway. Our 9:16 with the top bar used for a title (V9K6 style) gets the same benefit and keeps the face-following pipeline.
- A fake social-post header that copies another account (2Z55's 'DETERMINED' card): the prompt idea is kept in change 2. A card that looks like someone else's post, or an official show account, could mislead. Use our own channel name or a plain title.
- Live viewer counters, stream overlays, and other channels' watermarks or logos (sUeJ, 5OUl, mIOt, II1F): these belong to their own sources, not to us.
- Raw, nearly uncut playback with a 9s silent tail (Dk4e): it fails Tko's rule of visible cuts and face following. Steal only its title bar.
- An emoji on the punchline (e9NR) and speaker colours on every word: interview and streamer habits that would look off on a TV scene.
- Gore pixelation (01AJW): only needed for live-action gore. Our current sources are cartoons and Curb, so there's no rule to add until it comes up.
